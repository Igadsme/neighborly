## What counsel needs to cover

Neighborly is a request-first local marketplace. People create accounts, post listings and requests, make offers, message, move a transaction through statuses, and review each other. Housing, jobs, services, and community posts are in the same product. v1 does not take payment inside the app, does not upload images, and does not call an AI model.

A real terms document has to decide, at least:

- Who operates the service and which laws govern it
- Eligibility and account responsibility
- What users may post, and what moderators may remove, hide, suspend, or restore
- That offers, messages, and reviews are between users
- That off-app payments (the in-product warning about Venmo, Zelle, and PayPal) are outside the product
- That there is no in-app payment, wallet, or payout in v1
- Acceptable use, including reports and blocks
- Disclaimers and liability limits counsel is willing to stand behind
- How the terms change, and that the placeholder in git is not notice to users
